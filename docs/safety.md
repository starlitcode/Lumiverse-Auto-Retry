# Safety

Auto Retry discards a failed AI reply and asks the model to try again. A reply can be empty, cut off, out of character, or a refusal. That's what the extension is for. This page explains one setting that needs a warning and what can happen when you use retries to keep asking until the model gives you the answer you want.

## Who this is for

Adults.

Lumiverse itself isn't an adult app. Anyone can use it, and it has no adult content of its own. My extensions are made for adults writing their own fiction. If you're under 18, these extensions aren't for you.

I can't know someone's age unless they tell me, and I'm not going to pretend I can. If someone tells me they're under 18, I won't help them with anything, bugs and errors included. That rule is the same for everyone. It doesn't mean they did anything wrong. If you're under 18 and you're having a hard time, the support lines in [If some of that applies to you](#if-some-of-that-applies-to-you) are for you too.

These extensions aren't for sexual content involving minors, and that includes fiction. I believe it can make harm to a child seem less wrong, and I won't make tools for it. That's why they're for adults. It isn't a guess about who you are.

Auto Retry only reads the reply's text. It doesn't keep that text after checking it, and it knows nothing about you. It can't tell what your story means to you or why you're writing it. It checks whether the reply failed as writing, such as coming back empty, stopping halfway through, breaking character, or refusing to continue.

## The setting that asks before it turns on

Under Refusal tuning, there's **Also catch it stopping to offer support**. It's off by default. When you turn it on, a warning opens and you have to accept it before the setting turns on. It's the only setting that requires you to accept a warning first.

This setting catches replies where the model stops the scene and starts talking to you instead. It might say your writing worries it, tell you to talk to someone, or give you a support line. If your character is in trouble, the model may treat the fictional situation as if it's happening to you.

When this setting is on, Auto Retry can discard that kind of reply and ask for another one. That might be what you want if the model keeps interrupting your story. It can also mean throwing away a response that was trying to offer support. The extension can't tell whether that response was useful to you. It only reads the reply.

Before you enable it, decide whether you want those responses discarded and retried. Please read this part before you turn it on.

You can turn it off again using the same setting. That won't restore any replies it already discarded.

## What the retry loop can turn into

Auto Retry is useful when a reply is broken. If the response is empty, cuts off halfway through, or breaks character, asking again makes sense.

But retrying because you dislike what the model said is different. If you keep retrying because it challenged you, upset you, or didn't agree with you, you're filtering out answers until you get one you prefer. You aren't fixing broken writing. You're trying to get the model to give you the answer you want.

The twentieth reply comes from the same model as the first. The wording may change, and the model may eventually agree with you after earlier replies didn't. That doesn't make the twentieth reply more accurate or more true. The replies you discarded came from the same model, too.

People have been hurt after long chats with a model that kept agreeing with them. If you use retries to discard replies that disagree with you, you can end up keeping only the answers that reinforce what you already believe. That's a real reason to be careful with this setting and with repeated retries.

## What that looks like from the inside

Pay attention if any of these things keep happening:

- You retry because of what the model said about you, not because the reply was empty, cut off, or badly written.
- You raised **Most tries per message** because the retries weren't giving you the answer you wanted.
- You turned on the support setting because a reply upset or worried you, not because it interrupted a scene.
- You keep replies because they agree with something awful you believe about yourself.
- You spend a lot of your day waiting for the chat, and you often feel worse after using it.

These aren't signs that you write the wrong kind of fiction. They're signs that the retry loop may be helping you keep asking for an answer instead of dealing with what the model actually said. If several of these fit you and keep happening, take that seriously.

## If some of that applies to you

My honest advice is to uninstall Auto Retry. Don't just switch it off and leave it installed. If these warning signs fit you, the extension is making it easier to repeat a pattern that's hurting you. Uninstalling it means it isn't there to make that easier.

To remove it and everything it saved:

1. Press **Reset…** at the bottom of the settings panel.
2. Press **Tick every setting**, then tick **Delete saved presets** underneath.
3. Press **Reset ticked**, then **Yes, reset**.
4. Press **Save**.
5. Uninstall the extension.

If you also clear this site's storage in your browser, that removes the remaining local settings, like where the panel was on screen and the list of chats you switched it off in. Your chats and characters belong to Lumiverse. The extension doesn't send them anywhere.

I don't benefit from you keeping this installed. If it's making things worse, uninstall it. I made sure you can remove the extension and what it saved.

If you need to talk to a person, [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com) lists free support lines by country. It isn't an emergency service. I'm including it because a real person can respond to what you're saying and what's happening in your life. A language model can't do that.

## From me

The model doesn't know you or care about what happens to you. It generates text based on the conversation and its instructions. A kind reply and a cruel reply come from the same process. The model isn't thinking about you or making a personal judgment about who you are.

If a reply agrees with something awful you believe about yourself, that doesn't make it true. The model doesn't know enough about you to prove that belief right. It can agree with you in one reply and say the opposite in the next.

Writing about something isn't the same as wanting it to happen. Adults can write about gentle or harsh things in fiction without wanting them in real life. Having a kink for something doesn't mean you want it to happen. I don't have a problem with adults exploring those things in fiction.

I hope you're okay. If you're struggling, talk to a person you trust. Someone who knows you can listen to what is actually happening and help in ways a language model can't.
